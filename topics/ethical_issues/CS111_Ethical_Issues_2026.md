## Why Discuss Ethics?

Technology gives us new capabilities, but being *able* to do something does not necessarily make it *right* to do it. For each situation, consider the people affected, the possible consequences, and whether changing one detail changes your judgment.

**Discussion prompt:** Ethical? Unethical? Or does it depend?

## Everyday Decisions and Responsible Technology Use

### Situation #1 — Using University Resources

A faculty member uses their office computer and university printer to print fliers for a garage sale.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- One flier? Ten? One hundred?
- Does the time of day matter?
- What if the fliers are for Sunday School or another community organization?
- What if they are for a political action group? Does the particular party matter?

### Situation #2 — Personal Phone Use at Work

An employee uses their personal cell phone to check text messages and social media during normal working hours.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the employee only checks during breaks?
- Ten minutes per day? Twenty? Thirty?
- What if personal phone use does not affect the employee's work?
- Would your answer change if the employee were working remotely?

### Situation #3 — Research, Writing, and AI Assistance

An HU student uses websites and generative AI tools while writing a research paper.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the student copies entire paragraphs from a website?
- What if the student rewrites or paraphrases paragraphs without citing the source?
- What if the student cites all sources?
- What if the student purchases a completed paper online?
- What if the student uses ChatGPT to suggest an outline or Grammarly to improve grammar?
- What if the student asks an AI tool to write the entire paper?

### Situation #4 — Software Failures and Patient Safety

A software bug causes an eye surgery machine to malfunction, injuring a patient.

- Who is responsible: the doctor, hospital, software company, programmer, or someone else?
- What if the hospital did not install an available software update?
- What if the problem was difficult to detect during testing?
- Would your answer change if the software used AI to recommend or control the procedure?

### Situation #5 — Online Relationships

A married person spends an hour each day chatting with an online friend, developing a close emotional relationship.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if they will never meet in person?
- What if the spouse knows? What if the conversations are kept secret?
- What makes an online relationship inappropriate?
- Would your answer change if the online friend were an AI companion?

### Situation #6 — Private Health Information

An employer obtains private health information about two job applicants without their permission. One applicant has a history of frequent doctor visits; the other is hired.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- Does the employer have a legitimate reason to know about the applicants' health?
- What if the employer claims the information predicts future costs or absences?
- Would it make a difference if an algorithm made the recommendation?
- What privacy or discrimination concerns arise?

### Situation #7 — Blocking Websites

HU uses filtering software to block websites labeled as “adult content.” A student cannot access a legitimate breast cancer research article.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- Who should decide what content is appropriate?
- Should there be a process for requesting access?
- Should university networks provide unrestricted access to the Internet?
- Should the government require age verfication for adult content?

### Situation #8 — A University Data Breach

A student worker learns that an unknown person downloaded a university database containing students' names and Social Security numbers. Months have passed, and students have not been notified.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- Who should be notified, and how quickly?
- What harms might occur even if no identity theft has yet been reported?
- Who is responsible for protecting the data and responding to the breach?

### Situation #9 — Sharing Software Subscriptions

An HU student shares an Adobe Creative Cloud subscription with a classmate, although the subscription is licensed for one individual.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if it is used only for course assignments?
- What if the classmate uses it only once or twice a month?
- What if the classmate cannot afford a subscription?
- Does agreeing to software license terms create an ethical obligation?
- Is it ethical to use AI generate software that is a "clone" of popular commercial applications?

### Situation #10 — Downloading Music and Digital Content

A student downloads copyrighted music without paying or receiving permission from the copyright holder.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the music is Christian music?
- What if the student would never have purchased it?
- What if the student already owns the CD?
- What if the song appears on YouTube or another streaming site?
- Does freely available content necessarily mean it is free to copy?

### Situation #11 — Using a Neighbor's Wi-Fi

A student moves into a summer rental and discovers that they can connect to a neighbor's unsecured wireless network.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the student uses it only a few minutes each day?
- What if the neighbor gives permission?
- What if the neighbor's Internet provider has rules against sharing the connection?

### Situation #12 — Copying a Digital Rental

A student rents a movie digitally for 48 hours but cannot watch it before the rental expires. They use software to save a permanent copy to their computer.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if they delete the copy immediately after watching it once?
- What if they share the copy with a friend?
- Is this different from recording a movie shown on television or saving content from a streaming service?

### Situation #13 — AI Completing Student Assignments

A college student uses ChatGPT to complete a programming assignment. The student reviews the generated code, understands how it works, and submits it as their own.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the student understands every line?
- What if the student modifies the code?
- What if the instructor permits AI assistance?
- What if the student does not disclose AI use?

### Situation #14 — AI-Generated Images and Deepfakes

A college student creates a realistic AI-generated video showing a professor making statements they never actually made. The student shares the video on social media.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the video is clearly labeled as AI-generated?
- What if it is intended as a joke?
- What if it damages the professor's reputation?
- What if similar technology is used in political advertising?

### Situation #15 — Sharing Private Information with AI

An employee uploads confidential company documents to a public AI chatbot to summarize the information and prepare a report.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if no one outside the company sees the finished report?
- What if the AI provider stores the uploaded information?
- What if the employee removes names and identifying information?
- What if the company has no policy concerning AI use?

### Situation #16 — Social Media and Addictive Algorithms

A social media company designs its recommendation algorithm to maximize the time users spend on the platform, even when the content may negatively affect their mental health.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if users voluntarily choose to use the platform?
- What if the company profits from advertising?
- What if the users are children or teenagers?
- Should companies prioritize user well-being over profits?

### Situation #17 — Discovering a Security Vulnerability

A computer science student discovers a security flaw in the university's website that could allow access to confidential student information.

- Is it ethical to investigate further?
- What if the student accesses private records to demonstrate the vulnerability?
- What if the student reports the problem privately?
- What if the university ignores the warning?
- Should the student publicly disclose the vulnerability?

### Situation #18 — Environmental Costs of AI

A technology company builds a large AI data center that consumes significant electricity and water resources but also brings jobs and economic development to a community.

- Ethical? Unethical?
- What if the community benefits economically?
- What if local electricity or water costs increase?
- What if the company uses renewable energy?
- Who should decide whether the benefits outweigh the costs?

## How Do We Decide What Is Ethical?

Possible influences on our decisions include:

- Written policies, laws, and procedures
- What others do (“going with the flow”)
- Opinions of friends, family, and colleagues
- Professional codes of ethics
- Personal and religious morals and values
- Biblical principles and Scripture

**Discussion:** Are these always consistent? What should we do when they conflict?

## Professional Codes of Ethics

Many professional organizations publish codes of ethical conduct. In computing, one important example is the **Association for Computing Machinery (ACM)**.

The [ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct (2018)](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics) includes seven general ethical principles. In summary, computing professionals should:

1. Contribute to society and human well-being.
2. Avoid harm.
3. Be honest and trustworthy.
4. Be fair and take action not to discriminate.
5. Respect the work required to produce new ideas, inventions, creative works, and computing artifacts.
6. Respect privacy.
7. Honor confidentiality.

**Discussion:** Which of these principles applies to the scenarios we discussed? What professional organizations publish ethical guidelines in your own discipline?

## What Is Truly Ethical?

- How can we get beyond our first reaction that something is “right” or “wrong”?
- *Why* is it right or wrong?
- What makes an issue a gray area?
- How will I live responsibly in a world filled with technology?
- What do our technology choices say about our faith as Christians?

## A Framework for Ethical Analysis

When considering an ethical situation, ask:

1. **Facts:** What do we know? What information is missing? What do credible sources say?
2. **Issues:** What ethical principles or competing interests are involved?
3. **People affected:** Who are the stakeholders? Who may benefit or be harmed?
4. **Options:** What choices are available?
5. **Consequences:** What are the possible short- and long-term results of those choices?
6. **Faith and values:** How do my values, religious beliefs, and biblical principles inform my decision?

**Final question:** What would *you* do, and why?
