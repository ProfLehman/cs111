## Project Overview

Working in groups of 3–4 students, select a current ethical issue related to computers and technology. Research the issue, perform an ethical analysis, and present your findings to the class.

The purpose of this project is to examine how technology affects individuals and society, consider different perspectives and possible consequences, and reflect on how personal values, Christian faith, and biblical principles can guide ethical decisions.

The project has two components:

1. **Group Presentation:** An 8–10 minute presentation examining a technology-related ethical issue.
2. **Individual Reflection:** A 400–500 word written reflection exploring your personal values, faith, and how you would respond to the issue.

### Ethical Analysis

Your group presentation must address the following questions:

1. **Facts:** What are the important facts surrounding the issue? Provide sufficient background for the class to understand the situation.

2. **Ethical Issues:** What are the primary ethical questions or concerns? Explain why this is an ethical issue rather than simply a technical problem.

3. **People and Groups Involved:** Who are the individuals, organizations, or groups affected by this issue?

4. **Impact:** How are these individuals or groups affected? Consider both positive and negative consequences.

5. **Options and Consequences:** What are the possible actions or responses? What consequences might result from these options for the different groups involved?

6. **Biblical Principles:** What scripture passages or biblical principles might apply to this ethical issue? Explain how these principles could help guide ethical decisions related to the issue. Include at least one relevant scripture passage or biblical principle.

7. **Recommendation:** Based on your analysis, what actions or responses does your group recommend? Explain your reasoning.

### Presentation Requirements

- Prepare an **8–10 minute group presentation**. Aim for approximately 8 minutes to allow time for questions.
- Allow approximately 2–3 minutes for questions and discussion and 1 minute for transitions. There will be a **12-minute maximum per group** to keep presentations on schedule.
- **Every group member must speak** and contribute meaningfully to the presentation.
- You may use PowerPoint, Google Slides, a web page, or another appropriate presentation format.
- Organize your presentation so classmates can understand the issue, different perspectives, relevant biblical principles, and your group's recommendation.
- Include at least **three credible references** supporting your analysis.
- Cite sources within your presentation and provide a reference list at the end. APA, MLA, or another consistent citation style is acceptable.
- Be prepared to answer questions from the instructor and classmates.

## Part 2 – Individual Reflection

Each student must independently submit a **400–500 word written reflection** examining the ethical issue from a personal perspective.

Your reflection should address the following questions:

1. **Personal Values and Beliefs:** How do your personal values and beliefs, including your religious faith, influence your understanding of this ethical issue? Have your views changed as a result of researching the topic?

2. **Biblical Principles:** What scripture passages or biblical principles apply to this ethical issue? Explain how they relate to the situation and might guide your response.

3. **Personal Response:** If confronted with this type of ethical situation, what would you do? Explain your decision and the reasoning behind it, including any challenges or consequences you might face.

Your reflection should be written in your own words and demonstrate thoughtful consideration of the issue. You do not need to agree with your group's recommendation or with your instructor's perspective.

You will be evaluated on the depth of your reflection and reasoning, not on whether you hold a particular opinion. **Do not use AI to write your individual reflection.**

## AI Usage – Use AI with Caution (Yellow)

Generative AI tools may be used to assist with research, brainstorming, organizing ideas, and reviewing presentation materials.

However:

- Your group must evaluate sources, develop its own ethical analysis, and understand all material presented.
- AI-generated information must be checked for accuracy, and references must be verified.
- The individual reflection must represent your own thinking, personal values, faith, and decisions. Do not use AI to write your individual reflection.
- All students must be prepared to explain and defend the ideas they present.
